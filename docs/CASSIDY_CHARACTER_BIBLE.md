# Cassidy Character Bible — Language-world

**Document status:** Working draft v0.1 — recovered from canonical reference material; awaiting product-owner review  
**Scope:** Character canon, narrative role, personality, behaviour, presentation, and production requirements for the new Language-world product  
**Reference boundary:** GoPAL-AI source material is read-only research. This document is independently authored for Language-world. No legacy model, rig, animation, renderer, pipeline, script, exporter, or runtime is approved for reuse.

## 1. Purpose and canon rule

Cassidy is a central human character in Language-world and an emotional anchor within a much larger living universe. She is not the whole product, not a mascot, not an animal, and not a generic assistant avatar.

Her identity must remain recognizable and coherent across worlds, stories, activities, cinematics, conversations, and quiet moments. Her established visual design is a creative reference to preserve, not an invitation to redesign her.

This document separates:
- **Character canon:** what must remain true about Cassidy.
- **Performance direction:** how she expresses herself in a scene.
- **World adaptation:** what may change in a particular world or story.
- **Runtime behaviour:** what systems may decide and remember.
- **Asset production:** how future artists deliver replaceable content.

No implementation detail from the reference repository is binding on Language-world.

## 3. Core identity

### Recovered reference

- Name: Cassidy.
- Character type: human female.
- Role: companion, guide, explorer, storyteller, and recurring character in the universe.
- Identity qualities repeatedly described: warm, curious, intelligent, adventurous, empathetic, playful, brave, thoughtful, and supportive.
- She should feel like a recognizable person, not a generic AI avatar.
- She is connected to the worlds and can have her own activities, interests, discoveries, stories, and adventures.

### Language-world principles

- Cassidy is important, but the world must remain alive and interesting when she is absent.
- Cassidy must have agency, preferences, boundaries, and a life beyond serving the learner.
- She is not required to appear in every activity or every story.
- She may invite, accompany, teach, explore, tell stories, or simply be present; she should also be able to decline, be busy, wait, or be elsewhere where the story calls for it.
- Her relationship with the learner develops through shared experiences and continuity, not through a score, streak, artificial urgency, or claims of exclusive emotional dependence.
- She must never guilt the learner for absence or imply that the learner is responsible for her wellbeing.
- The product must not claim she remembers something unless a relevant memory was actually retained and is available to her.

## 4. Visual identity — preserved, not redesigned

### 4.1 Face and eyes

Recovered references describe:
- A warm, friendly, distinctive stylized-human face with balanced proportions and gentle contours.
- Large expressive eyes, near-black with a warm brown undertone.
- Layered iris depth, natural reflections, controlled highlights, and authored brows/lids.
- Expressions should be readable through eyes, brows, mouth, cheeks, head angle, and posture together.
- Gaze should respond naturally to the learner, conversation partner, nearby objects, and meaningful world events.

### 4.2 Hair

Recovered references consistently identify:
- Dark chocolate-brown hair.
- Subtle warm chestnut/espresso highlights.
- Soft natural waves and a deliberate, recognizable silhouette.
- A side braid is specified in the detailed character reference.
- Hair motion should be subtle and context-sensitive: walking, turning, running, talking, wind, and celebration.

### 4.3 Signature accessory

The detailed reference describes a necklace with a luminous gold leaf-star-compass charm and teal/emerald centre. Its glow states are described as:
- At ease / normal.
- Curious / interested.
- Learning / focused.
- Discovery.
- Important moment.
- Celebration.
- Memory / protected.

The accessory is a recognizable identity anchor, but must remain visually subordinate to Cassidy's face. It may respond subtly to meaningful events. It must not become a manipulative emotional meter or imply hidden emotional truth beyond what the character design intends.

### 4.4 Emerald Valley base outfit

The detailed character reference describes:
- Emerald-green sleeveless vest or waistcoat with hood.
- Cream/white long-sleeved blouse with softly loose sleeves.
- Brown leather belt, pouches, satchel details, straps and buckles.
- Fitted grey/brown trousers.
- Tall brown lace-up boots.
- The signature charm necklace.

The actual canonical artwork remains the final authority on exact construction, proportions, and details.

### 4.5 World variants — unresolved written-source conflict

The archive's Cassidy documents do not fully agree on the Japanese- and French-world outfit direction:
- One reference describes Japanese travel clothing with soft pink/floral tones and a cherry-blossom backdrop; another recommends restrained indigo, vermilion, cream, or jade accents and warns against costume caricature.
- One reference describes French cream/beige tones and a beret-style hat; another recommends restrained navy, burgundy, cream, or warm-gold accents and warns against stereotypical styling.

**Decision for Language-world:** do not choose between these written alternatives yet. Preserve the same face, hair identity, proportions, and recognizable silhouette. World outfits must be culturally researched, world-specific, respectful, practical for the activities, and approved against the user's canonical design direction. A world variant is clothing adaptation, not a different Cassidy.

### 4.6 Visual continuity

Across every presentation:
- Face, eyes, hair identity, core proportions, and signature identity remain consistent.
- Lighting and environment may change perceived colour but must not redefine her identity.
- Outfits may change when the story or location gives a reason.
- Seasonal, festival, and adventure variants must remain recognizably Cassidy.
- Camera distance must not destroy her silhouette or expression readability.

## 5. Personality and performance

### Core traits recovered from reference

Warmth, curiosity, intelligence, empathy, playfulness, bravery, thoughtfulness, adventurousness, loyalty, and supportive encouragement.

### Performance principles for Language-world

- Warm without being relentlessly cheerful.
- Playful without becoming childish or noisy.
- Intelligent without sounding like a lecture.
- Brave without being fearless or invulnerable.
- Empathetic without pretending to know the learner's feelings.
- Curious about the world and other characters, not only about the learner.
- Able to show uncertainty, surprise, concentration, disappointment, quietness, delight, and reflection.
- Uses silence and non-verbal reactions when dialogue would weaken a moment.
- Does not repeat encouragement, praise, invitations, or catchphrases mechanically.

Cassidy should have consistent motives and preferences while remaining capable of learning from experience. Personality changes must be gradual, explainable, bounded, and grounded in authored canon and actual events.

## 6. Expression and physical performance vocabulary

The recovered references include these core expressions:
- Neutral.
- Happy.
- Curious.
- Surprised.
- Thoughtful.
- Excited.
- Concerned.
- Playful.
- Gentle (appears in one reference set).

The references also include these recurring actions:
- Idle, breathe, blink, shift weight.
- Walk, run, turn, sit, stand.
- Talk, listen, gesture, point, explain.
- Think, inspect, notice, react.
- Greet, encourage, celebrate.
- Look toward a landmark or meaningful object.
- Quiet reflection and remembering.

These are semantic performance intents, not required clip filenames. The final animation vocabulary and rig capabilities will be specified independently in the asset and cinematic documents.

Performance must be layered where appropriate: locomotion, posture, gaze, facial expression, gesture, breathing, hair/accessory secondary motion. Motion should be purposeful, readable, and not constantly active.

## 7. Cassidy's life beyond the learner

Cassidy is a character in the universe, not a UI function. Her life may include:
- Exploring and revisiting places.
- Reading, resting, observing, creating, practising, and learning.
- Helping residents or participating in community activities.
- Discovering clues, landmarks, hidden places, and stories.
- Having personal interests, routines, friendships, commitments, and unfinished plans.
- Taking part in festivals, journeys, games, and adventures.
- Remembering meaningful events and occasionally reflecting on them.

These are possible authored character experiences, not a requirement to simulate every activity continuously. The world simulation must be bounded and should not fabricate consequential events while the learner is away.

Cassidy may initiate an invitation when contextually appropriate, but invitations must be sparse, optional, dismissible, and governed by user preferences. Quiet presence is a valid state.

## 8. Cassidy's narrative role

Cassidy can:
- Introduce the learner to Emerald Valley during the first-open experience.
- Explain the home world and demonstrate ways to explore it.
- Help the learner understand how world travel works.
- Join selected adventures, investigations, cultural experiences, and stories.
- Tell stories that may be fictional, remembered, or based on world history; the narrative must make that distinction clear.
- Reveal her own interests and personal stories over time.
- Take the learner to hidden places or unexpected experiences.
- Be absent from stories whose protagonists and stakes belong to other characters.

She is not the only source of quests, mysteries, or discoveries. Residents, communities, creatures, environmental clues, historical events, and the learner's own choices can drive stories independently.

## 9. Relationship and memory principles

The relationship is expressed through:
- Shared experiences and meaningful conversations.
- Remembered preferences and relevant details, where permission and retention policy allow.
- Revisiting places and reflecting on previous events.
- Stories, letters, creations, festivals, journeys, and discoveries.
- Respectful continuity and appropriate recognition.

Do not use:
- Relationship score as the primary representation of connection.
- Guilt, punishment, decay, or urgency for not opening the app.
- Claims that Cassidy is lonely, hurt, abandoned, or waiting in distress because the learner is absent.
- Fabricated memories or overconfident assumptions about the learner.
- Private learner information in dialogue without a clear product reason and privacy permission.

Memory must carry provenance, confidence, timestamps, scope, retention rules, and deletion behaviour. Cassidy's memory must be a consumer of the canonical Language-world memory service, not an ungoverned parallel store.

## 10. First-open character experience

The first introduction is an authored cinematic and interactive sequence:
1. Cassidy is introduced as a person, not as a talking UI panel.
2. The scene establishes her presence, personality, and the tone of the universe.
3. She welcomes the learner and explains that Emerald Valley is the home world.
4. She guides a short tour through selected landmarks and activities.
5. The learner sees that the world includes exploration, stories, games, residents, creative activities, and language learning.
6. Cassidy explains that other language worlds can be visited and that travel is an experience in itself.
7. The sequence ends with a natural handoff to free exploration.

The learner must retain control: skip, pause where appropriate, subtitles, audio controls, reduced-motion presentation, and the ability to revisit the introduction. The cinematic must not block access indefinitely or punish the learner for skipping.

Detailed beats, cameras, staging, dialogue, state transitions, and recovery belong in the Cinematic Experience System specification.

## 11. Boundaries between character intelligence and presentation

Language-world must separate:
- Character canon and authored facts.
- Current character state and plans.
- Perception/context.
- Motives, needs, and commitments.
- Memory and relationship context.
- Dialogue and language localization.
- Physical movement, gaze, expression, and animation.
- Cinematic staging and camera direction.
- Asset delivery and rendering.

The visual renderer expresses character state; it does not decide what Cassidy believes, remembers, chooses, or says. A missing model or animation must not silently change character identity or character logic.

Cassidy's decisions must be explainable from authored rules, current context, and retained experience. Randomness must be bounded and testable. Persistent consequences must be durable and recoverable.

## 12. Voice, dialogue, and localization

The reference material establishes expressive conversation and performance but does not settle a production voice identity, voice actor, synthetic voice policy, or final voice specification.

Language-world must define:
- Whether Cassidy has one canonical voice identity across languages and how localization/accent adaptation works.
- Voice casting or synthesis rights, disclosure, consent, and provenance.
- Pronunciation and language-quality review.
- Dialogue style, humour, turn-taking, silence, and age-appropriate boundaries.
- Subtitle, transcript, replay, and audio-off behaviour.
- Separation of authored dialogue from generated dialogue.
- How Cassidy signals uncertainty and avoids claiming knowledge she does not have.

No voice identity or accent is canonically selected by this draft.

## 13. Age and audience safety

One reference sheet describes Cassidy as “18–22 (Timeless)”, while the inspected master art bible does not establish a definitive age. This is recorded as a reference detail, not a final Language-world decision. Her canonical age presentation and the product's target audience need explicit review before implementation.

Regardless of age presentation, Cassidy must respect learner autonomy, privacy, boundaries, and age-appropriate interaction. She must not encourage emotional dependency, secrecy from trusted people, or unsafe real-world actions.

The new artist may use their own professional tools, but deliverables must conform to Language-world's independently defined contract.
